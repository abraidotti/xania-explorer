    Nothing new to learn after level 70¶

    New abilities arrive steadily up to level 60, where most classes get a burst: many other classes' spells open to everyone at 60 (a barbarian gains 49 at once). After that the list is finished: the last new ability comes at level 60 for mages, 60 for clerics, 70 for knights, 70 for barbarians, so the final 21–31 levels bring nothing new to learn.

    Practices only catch up with the default package by level 79¶

    Practising raises a skill by int_app.learn / difficulty, so taking a default-package skill from 1% to the 75% adept limit costs a handful of practices each. Practices arrive slowly (wis-based, often 1–3 a level), so by level 20 a mage needs 112 but has 33, a cleric needs 181 but has 62, a knight needs 218 but has 41 and a barbarian needs 196 but has 41. Supply catches up with demand for good at level 58 (mage), 77 (cleric), 79 (knight), 74 (barbarian). In between, players must choose what to practise; using a skill also improves it, which softens this.

    A full mana bar buys about 1.1 kills, at every level¶

    Spending a full mana bar on a mage's best attack spell kills 0.4–1.7 level-appropriate mobs (median 1.1) at every level from 10 up: mana growth (see Characters), falling mana cost and rising spell damage roughly cancel mob HP growth. A caster fighting with spells alone needs to regenerate its mana after about every kill.

    Attack spells fall behind mob HP: 41 casts per kill by level 91¶

    A mage's best spell does about 105 damage per cast at level 30 (acid blast) and 192 at 91, a 1.8× rise. Over the same levels the median level-appropriate mob goes from 1,014 to 7,809 HP, 7.7× more. The damage tables in magic.cpp flatten out after the 30s, so the number of casts per kill climbs from about 10 to 41.

    Casters out-damage everyone, until the mana runs out¶

    Per round, a mage's best spell does 3.9× its melee damage at level 30 and 2.7× at 91, and out-damages even a knight's melee (131 per round at 91) for as long as the mana lasts. Casters are strong in bursts and weak over a long fight, which is exactly where the long mob-HP fights of the Combat page hurt them.

    Method¶

    How these numbers are calculated

    Model (human, default package; see Characters): - Available = skills/spells the class can learn by that level (skill_table level for the class, rating > 0). Skills outside the default package must be bought later with trains (gain). - Practice budget: practices earned (5 at creation + wis-based per level) against the practices needed to take every default-package skill available so far from 1% to the 75% adept limit, at int_app.learn / difficulty per practice (act_info.cpp:1604). - Attack spells: the best direct-damage spell in the class's default package at each level, cast at the caster's level against a same-level mob (50% save chance), at the 75%→100% skill assumption used for Combat.

    • Expected spell damage = mean of the table roll (½× to 2× the table value) × (1 − save chance/2) × cast success (skill %). Mana per cast = max(min_mana, 100 / (2 + level − level learned)) (magic.cpp:582). A cast lags the caster for the spell's beats; damage per round scales by 12 / beats.
    • Mob HP = the median combat mob within ±2 levels (as in Combat).
    • Healing, buffs, debuffs and area spells aren't valued here; chain lightning counts its first target only.
